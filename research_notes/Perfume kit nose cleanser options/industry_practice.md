# Industry practice for resetting the nose between fragrance smells

Scope: perfumers/evaluators, retail counters, at-home discovery and quiz kits, professional sensory panels, and commercial "nose reset" products. Research date: 2026-10-03. About 25 searches and fetches. Several primary pages (Osmoz, Perfumer & Flavorist, Style Girlfriend) returned HTTP 403 and could not be read in full. Those are flagged below.

## 1. What professional perfumers and evaluators do between smells

### Takeaway
Named perfumers and the trade press say the same things: smell your own clean skin (inside of the arm, crook of the elbow, back of the hand), take breaks with fresh air, and limit how many scents you smell per session (about 3 to 7). None of the people I could identify recommend coffee beans. I found no published, named protocol from Givaudan, IFF, dsm-firmenich, Symrise, or ISIPCA that specifies a reset object.

### Cited Findings
- Romano Ricci (founder and artistic director, Juliette Has a Gun) advises "limiting your testing to three to four scents and spreading your sniffs out by about 10 minutes." Business Insider, Apr 29 2025, updated Nov 19 2025, syndicated by AOL — [AOL / Business Insider](https://www.aol.com/articles/12-tips-finding-signature-scent-135702886.html)
- Brianna Arps (founder/perfumer, Moodeaux), in the same article, says coffee beans "might do more harm than good by clouding your nose's judgment." She recommends smelling clean skin on the inside of the arm or the back of the hand between fragrances — [AOL / Business Insider](https://www.aol.com/articles/12-tips-finding-signature-scent-135702886.html)
- Olfactory NYC, a perfume bar and fragrance-creation studio, published "No, Coffee Beans Don't Reset Your Palate." A search summary of the industry consensus says perfumers sniff the crook of the elbow, or an unscented shirt sleeve or skin. Not fetched in full — [Olfactory NYC](https://www.olfactorynyc.com/blogs/news/no-coffee-beans-don-t-reset-your-palate); [Air Aroma](https://www.air-aroma.com/?p=566)
- Connection Parfumerie (Australian niche retailer, Dec 11 2025) says "most professional perfumers do not use coffee beans" but names none. It recommends own skin (wrist or forearm), breaks, fresh air, 3 to 5 fragrances per session, and light sniffs rather than deep inhalations. It says stores keep coffee beans "because customers expect them" — [Connection Parfumerie](https://connectionparfumerie.com.au/blogs/news/how-to-reset-your-nose-properly-the-science-behind-smelling-clearly-in-perfumery)
- Perfumer & Flavorist ran a trade article on olfactory fatigue in product testing. Per the search snippet it gives 5 to 7 samples per session, at least 10 minutes rest between groups, and says rest of "2-5 minutes appear[s] to be sufficient to insure recovery of sensitivity at supraliminal intensity levels." The page returned 403, so I could not verify the exact text — [Perfumer & Flavorist](https://www.perfumerflavorist.com/fragrance/regulatory-research/article/21855850/olfactory-fatigue-what-it-is-and-how-to-avoid-it-in-product-testing)
- ISIPCA (Versailles; founded 1970 at Jean-Jacques Guerlain's initiative) teaches raw-material olfaction and fragrance evaluation. Its public course pages do not describe a reset method — [ISIPCA Scent Design & Creation](https://www.isipca.fr/en/formations/perfume/scent-design-creation); [Nez: where to train as a perfumer](https://mag.bynez.com/en/reports/olfactory-education-how-to-cultivate-your-nose/where-to-train-as-a-perfumer/)
- Basenotes has forum threads where perfumers and hobbyists discuss technique ("do perfumers have a technique", "noseblindness when formulating"). These are community anecdote, not industry protocol, and were not fetched — [Basenotes thread 1](https://basenotes.com/threads/strange-question-do-perfumers-have-a-technique.257181/); [Basenotes thread 2](https://basenotes.com/threads/noseblindness-when-formulating.551631/)

### Inferences
- In the professional consensus, the "reset" is mostly time, a clean air break, and your own neutral skin, not an object. A kit item that turns those three into a ritual (a timer cue, an instruction to smell the inner elbow, a "step to the window" prompt) would match pro practice better than a coffee jar.
- Numbers to build a 30-minute kit around: 3 to 4 scents (Ricci) up to 5 to 7 (P&F); 2 to 5 minutes of recovery between scents (P&F snippet), or about 10 minutes spacing (Ricci). Four scents at roughly 5 to 7 minutes each fits 30 minutes.

### Gaps
- I found no on-the-record statement from evaluators at Givaudan, IFF, dsm-firmenich, or Symrise about what they smell between submissions. A search snippet mentioned a Givaudan 2026 Evaluation Training Programme (24 evaluators, two weeks), but I could not find the source page or any details on reset methods.
- I found no named source for the "wool sleeve" technique attributed to perfumers. It appears only as unattributed retail-blog advice (see section 2).

## 2. Retail practice: coffee beans and their alternatives

### Takeaway
Coffee-bean jars are still the default at department-store, Sephora-style, and duty-free counters. A growing number of niche retailers and brands publicly reject them in their content (2025 to 2026), but I found no documented case of a major chain formally removing them. Wool or cotton swatches are mentioned as a boutique alternative, but I could not attribute that to a named retailer.

### Cited Findings
- The main study: Grosofsky, A., Haupert, M.L., & Versteeg, S.W. (2011), "An exploratory investigation of coffee and lemon scents and odor identification," *Perceptual and Motor Skills* 112(2): 536–538, DOI 10.2466/24.PMS.112.2.536-538 — [Wikipedia: Olfactory fatigue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olfactory_fatigue)
  - Method: college students rated 3 of 4 drugstore "Designer Imposter" perfumes on blotters, then sniffed coffee beans, lemon slices, or plain air, then had to identify the new 4th perfume.
  - Results: 57% correct after air, 62% after coffee, 86% after lemon. The differences were not statistically significant.
  - Authors' conclusion: "Coffee beans and lemon seem to have no special refreshing properties." They suggested "fragrance sellers may wish to reconsider the practice of providing coffee beans" — [First Nerve (Avery Gilbert, smell scientist), May 10 2011](https://www.firstnerve.com/2011/05/coffee-bean-meme-olfactory-palate.html)
  - Caveat: one small exploratory study with student subjects. "No significant difference" means unproven, not proven harmful. The lemon result (86%) is suggestive but not significant.
- Other critiques of the practice: [Volatile Fiction, "Ditch the coffee beans," Oct 2014](https://volatilefiction.wordpress.com/2014/10/21/ditch-the-coffee-beans-why-the-popular-perfume-sales-meme-is-wrong/); [Undina's Looking Glass, Jul 2011](https://undina.com/2011/07/17/coffee-anyone/); [Gustavus psychology blog](https://psychologicalscience.blog.gustavus.edu/?p=750)
- Brand and retailer content rejecting beans, 2025 to 2026:
  - [Fragrance Forte UK, "Coffee Beans Don't Actually Reset Your Nose"](https://fragranceforte.co.uk/blogs/news/fun-fact-7-coffee-beans-don-t-actually-reset-your-nose)
  - [Smell Stories (Belgium), "The coffee bean myth"](https://www.smellstories.be/en/blogs/blog/the-coffee-bean-myth-sniffing-out-the-truth/)
  - [Buchart Colbert journal, ©2026](https://buchartcolbert.com/blogs/journal/why-do-we-smell-coffee-beans-between-perfumes): recommends "fresh air, neutral scents, and short breaks," unscented fabrics, your own skin, or breathing deeply with eyes closed. It cites no study. The brand pushes its at-home Sample Discovery Set as a way to test slowly instead.
  - [Connection Parfumerie, Dec 2025](https://connectionparfumerie.com.au/blogs/news/how-to-reset-your-nose-properly-the-science-behind-smelling-clearly-in-perfumery)
- Retail-blog claims, single source or unattributed:
  - Some boutiques offer "pieces of wool or cotton fabric" instead of beans.
  - Store beans are "often old, stale, and saturated with perfume."
  - Both claims come from coffee and retail explainer blogs; the search summary did not make clear which page said what — [Scent Apprentice](https://scentapprentice.com/coffee-beans-in-perfume-stores/); [Barista HQ](https://baristahq.com/why-are-there-coffee-beans-in-perfume-store/); [Fragrance Outlet](https://www.fragranceoutlet.com/blogs/article/why-are-there-coffee-beans-in-the-perfume-store)
- Osmoz (owned by Firmenich and dsm-firmenich) has an explainer, "Why are there coffee beans in perfume store?", with a title framing them as "Really helpful to neutralize smell." It returned 403, so the content is unverified. Note that a fragrance-house-owned consumer site appears to endorse the practice — [Osmoz](https://www.osmoz.com/inspiration/questions-selections/567/why-there-coffee-beans-perfume-store)

### Inferences
- Beans persist because they act as a ritual and expectation cue ("because customers expect them," per Connection Parfumerie). Grosofsky's own data do not show beans harming identification. A premium kit can drop beans and point to the science, but the cleanser should still give the user an action to perform, or it will feel like something is missing.
- Odourless ceramic or stone, unscented wool, and plain cotton all fit the "neutral fabric/skin" logic that retailers cite. None has controlled-study support beyond "it doesn't add a competing odour."

### Gaps
- I found no news coverage of Sephora, Ulta, DFS, Harrods, Lane Crawford, Joyce, or Hong Kong duty-free removing or keeping beans as a policy. The Sephora search returned only coffee-note perfumes.
- I found no named brand that sells or hands out a branded "scent reset card," odourless ceramic piece, or wool swatch at the counter.

### Takeaway
Mainstream luxury discovery sets (Byredo, Le Labo, Maison Margiela Replica) sell vials, and some include a redemption voucher. I found no evidence that any of them include a nose-reset item. The one probable example of a quiz-style kit with a reset item is Phlur, whose at-home test was reported to include coffee beans (secondary source, unverified). ### Inferences
- I found no premium discovery kit with a designed, branded reset object. For the YAT Element kit, a well-designed cleanser item plus wording grounded in the science ("your own skin is the most neutral reference you have") would be distinctive.

  - "nose reset"
  - "return to baseline"
  - "neutral reference"

## 4. Professional sensory panel practice (food, wine, coffee, odour labs)

### Takeaway
Formal sensory guidance accepts several neutralisers: lemon-juice solution, a small jar of coffee beans, or sniffing your own body odour at the elbow joint. It pairs them with strict limits on sample count (7 to 9 maximum) and timing. Fresh or clean air breaks and rest intervals are the common baseline. Coffee is tolerated in some lab protocols, but it is not the only method and is not mandatory.

### Cited Findings
- DLG (German Agricultural Society) Expert Report 01/2017, "Panel training on odour and aroma perception for sensory analysis":
  - Neutralisation "can be carried out by brief smelling of a lemon juice solution (provided the analysts are not testing citrus products and fragrances), by smelling a small glass container filled with coffee beans, or by sniffing the analyst's own body smell, e.g. in the elbow joint."
  - "The number of samples should not exceed seven to nine in order not to over-stress the olfactory system."
  - Testing time should be brief because some substances cause quick adaptation, and open jars lose concentration.
  - [DLG Expert Report 01/2017](https://www.dlg.org/en/mediacenter/dlg-expert-reports/food-sensory-technology/dlg-expert-report-01-2017-panel-training-on-odour-and-aroma-perception-for-sensory-analysis)
- Search snippets from industry and food-lab protocols (attribution among the linked documents not verified) mention:
  - about 1 minute between sniffs
  - "panelist breathes clean air for at least 20 seconds between samples"
  - a 5-minute break after every 10 to 12 samples
  - "clear the nose by smelling ground coffee, if necessary"
  - panelists smelling the back of the hand, with water between taste samples
  - [Corn Refiners odour/flavour panel PDF](https://corn.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/06/Odor_Flavor.pdf); [EU Erasmus+ Sensory Analysis Handbook 2018](https://ec.europa.eu/programmes/erasmus-plus/project-result-content/5849bdbf-8ca0-4b9d-b36e-668b38dd8912/Sensory-Assessment-ENG.pdf)
- ISO 13301:2018 covers odour, flavour, and taste detection thresholds using a 3-AFC procedure. The free abstract does not describe a reset method, and the full text is paywalled — [AFNOR NF ISO 13301](https://www.boutique.afnor.org/en-gb/standard/nf-iso-13301/sensory-analysis-methodology-general-guidance-for-measuring-odour-flavour-a/fa040380/20795)
- ISO 16000-30:2014 covers sensory testing of indoor air by human panels. Reset details are paywalled — [ISO 16000-30](https://www.iso.org/standard/55558.html)

### Inferences
- Sensory science supports the elbow/skin method alongside coffee and lemon. All three are "accepted" practice, but only the skin method adds no foreign odour. Lemon is explicitly excluded when testing fragrances or citrus. This supports a skin-first or neutral-object approach for a perfume kit.
- The 7-to-9 sample ceiling and the short-exposure guidance support a kit with no more than about 4 to 6 scents in 30 minutes.

### Gaps
- I could not access SCA coffee-cupping protocols or WSET/sommelier guidance on resetting between aromas. My understanding is that cupping uses water and breaks, and wine tasters often smell their own forearm, but I found no source for either in this session.
- I found no textbook citation (e.g., Meilgaard, Civille & Carr, *Sensory Evaluation Techniques*). The full text was not accessible.

## 5. Commercial "olfactory reset" / "nose cleanser" products and their claims

### Takeaway
Dedicated nose-reset products are rare and make marketing claims with no evidence. The commodity option is coffee-bean jars. I found one 2026-era branded "sensory reset" product (LuxReset Pod) with neurological claims and no citations or ingredient disclosure.

### Cited Findings
- LuxReset Pod sells "Starter Ritual Kits," "Refill Pods," and a subscription called "The Reset Continuum."
  - It frames sensory reset as "not a lifestyle concept. It is a neurological one" and talks about "sensory baseline" and "olfactory intervals."
  - The site gives no ingredients, no material composition, no price on the about page, no location, and no cited studies.
  - An image filename suggests February 2026.
  - Pure marketing claims — [LuxReset Pod](https://www.luxresetpod.com/about-4)
- Coffee-bean jars and bottles sold specifically "to rest your sense of smell for perfume test" are commodity Amazon items — [Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/clp/B09CLD9NYG)
- Smell-training kits (e.g., Le Nez du Café, a 36-vial coffee aroma kit) exist for training, not for resetting — [Espresso Parts](https://www.espressoparts.com/products/le-nez-du-cafe-the-scent-of-coffee-revelation-kit-36-scents-and-booklet)
- Evidence base: the only controlled study I found on a reset object (Grosofsky et al. 2011) found no significant benefit for coffee or lemon over air. Its small sample means "no proven benefit," not "proven useless" — [First Nerve](https://www.firstnerve.com/2011/05/coffee-bean-meme-olfactory-palate.html)
- A separate peer-reviewed study looks at short-term changes in smell and taste sensitivity after *drinking* coffee. It is not about sniffing beans, but it shows coffee interacts with chemosensory sensitivity in complex ways — [PMC7230594](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7230594/)

### Inferences
- A YAT cleanser should be positioned as a ritual and pacing tool ("pause, breathe, return to your own skin"), not as a receptor-clearing device. Neurological claims would be unsupported and could create regulatory and credibility risk for a premium brand.
- Defensible design directions:
  - unscented natural wool or linen swatch ("neutral reference")
  - glazed or unglazed odourless ceramic or stone (unglazed ceramic is porous and may pick up perfume over time; glazed is easier to keep neutral, an inference not tested here)
  - a card instructing the user to smell the inner elbow, plus a timed breathing pause
  - fresh coffee beans in a sealed pouch, as an expectation-matching fallback, flagged honestly as "tradition"

### Gaps
- I found no published Secundo & Sobel (2006) abstract on coffee and olfactory habituation. It is often cited in the trade, but I could not access or verify it, so it is excluded.
- I found no Asian or Hong Kong "nose cleanser" product.
- I found no independent testing of LuxReset Pod.
