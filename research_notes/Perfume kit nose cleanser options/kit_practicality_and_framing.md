# Practicality, Safety, Cost and Honest Framing of "Nose Reset" Items for a Boxed At-Home Perfume Test Kit (Hong Kong)

Context: small premium box also holding coded scent vials, blank scent strips, oil-blotting sheets, pH strips, an unscented test solution, and a printed lid used for photo-based measurement. Candidates: coffee (beans/grounds, sealed pouch), wool/cashmere swatch, unscented cotton/felt pad, activated charcoal sachet, odorless ceramic/stone, sealed "fresh air"/sniff cup, unscented paper card, distilled water mist, lemon/citrus (dried peel or scented strip), and "smell your own unscented skin".

Note on evidence base: the efficacy evidence is thin. The only controlled study found that tested coffee beans for perfume discrimination found no significant benefit over plain air. That one finding shapes the honest-framing section.

---

## 1. Does any candidate actually "reset" the nose? (efficacy baseline that drives framing)

### Takeaway
The one controlled study found (Grosofsky et al., Beloit College, *Perceptual and Motor Skills*, n=63) shows coffee beans and lemon were **not statistically better than sniffing plain air**. Olfactory recovery is driven mainly by time away from the odor (mean about 3 minutes, highly variable). A "nose reset" item therefore can only honestly be sold as a ritual or pause cue, not as a cleanser.

### Cited Findings
- In the Grosofsky study, students correctly identified a new perfume 57% of the time after sniffing plain air, 62% after coffee beans and 86% after lemon slices. The differences between treatments were **not statistically significant**. The authors concluded coffee beans did not raise accuracy and suggested fragrance sellers "reconsider" offering them. Total n=63. The perfumes were "Designer Imposter" dupes. — [First Nerve (Avery Gilbert) summary](https://www.firstnerve.com/2011/05/coffee-bean-meme-olfactory-palate.html)
- Avery Gilbert (smell scientist, First Nerve blog) says there is no scientific explanation for why coffee's hundreds of volatile compounds would reset smell. — [First Nerve](https://www.firstnerve.com/2011/05/coffee-bean-meme-olfactory-palate.html)
- Fragrance-community debate on coffee beans is long-running and unresolved. — [Basenotes "coffee bean debate" thread](https://basenotes.com/threads/the-coffee-bean-debate.182109/); [Undina blog on olfactory fatigue](https://undina.com/2011/07/17/coffee-anyone/)
- Philpott et al. (2008), *J Laryngol Otol* 122(9):912-917: in 14 healthy volunteers tested with phenethyl alcohol, mean olfactory clearance was **170 s (range 81-750 s)**. Times varied widely within and between individuals and were associated with age, symptoms and mood. The authors recommend a **15-minute wait** before retesting in clinical practice. — [UEA ePrints](https://ueaeprints.uea.ac.uk/id/eprint/13438/)
- A brand blog claims that more than half of sensitivity returns within about 2 minutes and nearly full function by about 5 minutes. It also says perfumers sniff the crook of the elbow because they are fully adapted to their own skin odor, which makes it a neutral reference. This is a commercial blog, not primary research. — [Premiere Peau blog](https://premierepeau.com/blogs/news/nose-blind-why-you-cant-smell-your-perfume-premiere-peau); see also [Salle Privée blog](https://www.salle-privee.com/blogs/stories/how-to-reset-your-sense-of-smell)

### Inferences
- The defensible functional "reset" is **time plus odor-free air**. The kit instruction ("pause 2-3 minutes; breathe normal room air or your own unscented skin") carries the real effect. Any physical object is a cue or prop that enforces the pause.
- The plain-air and own-skin options are the most evidence-consistent. They also add zero contamination risk and zero cost.
- Lemon's 86% was nominally the highest score, but it was not significant in a small sample, so no claim can rest on it.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve the original Grosofsky paper (volume, pages, p-values). Only the secondary summary was accessed.
- I found no controlled studies on wool, charcoal, ceramic or water mist as olfactory "resets".

## 2. Odor migration risk inside a closed box (coffee, citrus, charcoal) and packaging that prevents it

### Takeaway
Coffee and citrus are high risks for contaminating paper strips, blotters, inserts and the printed lid over weeks in HK humidity. Paper and board readily sorb odors, and higher humidity increases VOC transfer. Freshly roasted coffee **degasses for about a month**, and standard one-way-valve coffee bags are designed to vent that gas, which would vent aroma into the kit. Only a **valveless, heat-sealed foil laminate pouch** (PET/Al/PE or OPP/Al/PE) or a **sealed glass vial** is credible containment. Activated charcoal is a strong VOC sink. It would not pull fragrance through sealed glass, but it could scavenge any leaked fragrance and the box's own odors, and it adds dust and messaging risk.

### Cited Findings
**Coffee off-gassing and packaging**
- About 1% of freshly roasted coffee's weight is CO2. Degassing from whole beans lasts **about one month**. Packaging solves this with a **one-way valve** that lets CO2 out and keeps oxygen from coming in. — [Smrke et al. 2022, *Food Packaging and Shelf Life* (ZHAW repository PDF)](https://digitalcollection.zhaw.ch/bitstream/11475/26687/3/2022_Smrke-etal_Effects-of-different-coffee-storage-methods-on-coffee-freshness_FPSL.pdf)
- In the same study, beans nitrogen-flushed in **OPP/Al/PE** foil bags with valves kept O2 under 0.5% and CO2 rose to about 77% over 9 weeks. The authors say "the packages had an effective barrier". — [Smrke et al. 2022](https://digitalcollection.zhaw.ch/bitstream/11475/26687/3/2022_Smrke-etal_Effects-of-different-coffee-storage-methods-on-coffee-freshness_FPSL.pdf)
- Multilayer laminates with aluminum foil or metallized film have low permeability to gases and help prevent aroma escape. — [Viking Masek on one-way valves](https://vikingmasek.com/packaging-machine-resources/packaging-machine-blog/are-one-way-valves-right-for-your-coffee-packaging-process) (industry blog)
- Retail PET/AL/PE heat-sealable foil pouches are marketed as barriers to oxygen, odor and light. — [Walmart listing, Muka mylar bags](https://www.walmart.com/ip/5672176351) (seller claim)

**Paper/board sorption (strips, blotting paper, inserts, printed lid)**
- Raising relative humidity **increased VOC transfer** from paper and board. GC-MS peak areas and the number of detected substances both rose, in line with human sensory tests. — [TU Dresden poster, "Effect of humidity on VOC desorption from paper and board"](https://tu-dresden.de/mn/chemie/lc/lc2/ressourcen/dateien/poster/Poster_Effect-of-humidity-on-VOC-desorption-from-paper-and-board.pdf)
- Paper "readily absorbs and retains odors". Paperboard may absorb odorants during storage and should be kept in an odor-free area before printing. — [Universidad de Zaragoza thesis/paper (Zaguán)](https://zaguan.unizar.es/record/163911/files/texto_completo.pdf); [Holmen Paper Academy on paperboard migration](https://www.holmen.com/en/board-and-paper/insights/paper-academy/food-contact-safety-and-migration-in-paperboard/) (attribution from search summaries, not fully read)

**Activated carbon as a fragrance sink**
- Activated carbon fiber (ACF) has VOC adsorption rates and capacities **1-3 orders of magnitude greater** than common indoor sink materials at 10-1000 mg/m³. Limonene isotherms were measured in passive and active modes. — [Purdue dissertation, ACF for indoor VOC removal](https://docs.lib.purdue.edu/dissertations/AAI3210745)
- Limonene and linalool (major fragrance terpenes) adsorb onto activated carbon and can be thermally desorbed or regenerated. — [FHNW publication record](https://irf.fhnw.ch/entities/publication/d107b19e-464b-45f1-8fc1-abbe261e4deb)
- A search result reported a maximum activated-carbon capacity of 3.9×10³ µg/g (about 3.9 mg/g) for linalool. I could not confirm which paper this came from, so treat it as unverified. — (unverified; surfaced alongside [RSC Environ. Sci.: Adv. 2026](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2026/va/d5va00247h))

### Inferences
- **Coffee in a valve bag is the worst option.** Valves exist to vent headspace, so coffee aroma would continuously enter the kit's air space and sorb into the blank strips, the oil-blotting sheets and the printed board, especially at HK's 78-83% RH. Contaminated strips would bias every scent test. Containment needs **pre-degassed beans** (rested more than 4 weeks) in a **valveless** foil pouch, heat-sealed, or in a screw-cap or crimped glass vial. Otherwise valveless pouches can puff from residual CO2.
- **Grounds are worse than beans.** They have more surface area, degas and stale faster, and spill fine particles.
- **Citrus-scented strips or dried peel** carry limonene-dominant volatiles, which are highly volatile and diffuse readily. Without the same foil or glass containment they would cross-contaminate. Even with containment, users opening the citrus pouch near the strips can transfer odor by hand.
- **Charcoal sachet:** sealed glass vials would not lose fragrance to charcoal through the glass. If a vial cap seal is imperfect, though, a charcoal sink keeps the headspace concentration near zero, which keeps the evaporation gradient high and could *accelerate* loss from a leaky vial. Charcoal could *protect* blank strips from stray odors, which is a genuine plus if the box is otherwise clean. Net effect: ambiguous. Testing would be needed before claiming either way.
- The **printed lid** used for photo measurement faces mainly an odor and color risk. Any oils from coffee or citrus contact could stain it or alter its color calibration (inference, not tested).
- **Simplest low-risk design:** no odorous item in the box at all. Use own skin or plain air, or an inert object (glazed ceramic, stone, glass cup), plus a desiccant-controlled, sealed inner bag for the paper components.

### Gaps
- I found no study measuring coffee or citrus odor transfer into paper inside retail gift boxes over weeks. The risk is inferred from paper-sorption and humidity data.
- Permeation rates for specific pouch laminates against terpenes were not found. A small in-house test (strips stored with and without pouched coffee for 4 weeks at 30 °C and 80% RH, then blind-sniffed) is recommended.
- I found no data on activated charcoal's effect on sealed glass fragrance vials in closed boxes.

## 3. Shelf life and degradation in HK heat and humidity

### Takeaway
HK averages **78% RH annually** and 81-83% from March to August, with mean July temperature of 28.9 °C (daily max 31.6 °C). That sits right at the 80% RH threshold where cellulosic materials (paper, cotton) can mold in stagnant air. Coffee aroma degrades within weeks even unopened. Wool is more mold-resistant than cotton or paper. Inert items (ceramic, stone, glass) have effectively unlimited shelf life.

### Cited Findings
- HK Observatory 1991-2020 normals: annual mean RH 78%. Monthly RH is 82% in March, 83% in April and May, 82% in June, 81% in July and August. Mean temperatures are 28.3-28.9 °C from June to August, with mean daily max 30.7-31.6 °C. — [HKO Monthly Meteorological Normals 1991-2020](https://www.hko.gov.hk/en/cis/normal/1991_2020/normals.htm)
- Stagnant air above 80% RH will support mold on cellulosics (cotton, linen). Wool and silk are affected above about 92%. At 80% RH and 80 °F, mold can flourish in about three weeks. Textile storage should stay below 65% RH, ideally 50-55%. — [Wikipedia: Mold control and prevention (library and archive)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mold_control_and_prevention_(library_and_archive)); [Smithsonian MCI, Mold and mildew on textiles](https://mci.si.edu/mold-and-mildew-textiles); [NPS Conserve O Gram 16/1](https://nps.gov/subjects/museums/upload/16-01_508.pdf) (thresholds aggregated via search summary)
- Coffee: methanethiol (a key freshness compound) fell to about **33% of its initial value in unopened, nitrogen-flushed foil bags** within the 9-week study. In opened packs it fell to about 15% within 8 weeks. The authors note "even the best packaging and oxygen-free conditions cannot prevent loss of freshness". In opened packs, O2 returned to ambient (21%) within about a week. — [Smrke et al. 2022](https://digitalcollection.zhaw.ch/bitstream/11475/26687/3/2022_Smrke-etal_Effects-of-different-coffee-storage-methods-on-coffee-freshness_FPSL.pdf)
- Industry sources say foil or valve bags keep coffee for "several weeks to a couple of months". — [Viking Masek](https://vikingmasek.com/packaging-machine-resources/packaging-machine-blog/are-one-way-valves-right-for-your-coffee-packaging-process) (industry blog)
- Bamboo charcoal bag sellers claim a lifespan of up to two years and monthly "reactivation" in sunlight. These are seller claims, not verified. — [UCHANGE promo listing](https://uchangepromo.com/products/nature-fresh-bamboo-charcoal-air-purifying-bags)

### Inferences (per item)
| Item | HK shelf-life outlook | Main failure mode |
|---|---|---|
| Coffee beans (foil, valveless, sealed) | Aroma noticeably faded within about 1-3 months; inventory must rotate | Staling (oxidation, loss of thiols); stale coffee smells flat or rancid, which undermines premium feel |
| Coffee grounds | Shorter than beans | Faster staling, fines, spill |
| Wool/cashmere swatch | Long; mold only above about 92% RH | Absorbs ambient and fragrance odors over time; may smell "lanolin/wet wool" in humidity (unsourced) |
| Cotton/felt pad, paper card | Moderate; mold-prone at 80%+ RH stagnant air | Mold; odor pickup; humidity-driven VOC exchange |
| Activated charcoal sachet | Long, but it saturates (adsorbs moisture and VOCs while in stock) | Arrives partly "spent"; dust |
| Ceramic/stone (glazed/dense) | Indefinite | Breakage (ceramic); unglazed porous ceramic can absorb odors (unsourced) |
| Sealed sniff cup (glass/PP) | Indefinite | None significant |
| Distilled water mist | Liquid in spray bottle; microbial growth possible once opened in warm conditions (unsourced) | Leakage onto strips or printed lid; adds a liquid SKU |
| Dried citrus peel | Oxidizes and fades; mold if not fully dried | Aroma loss, mold, cross-contamination |
| Own skin | N/A | None |

- A **silica gel or clay desiccant** in the kit's inner bag is advisable for all paper items (strips, blotting sheets, pH strips, printed lid). Desiccants also adsorb some VOCs. This is an inference; verify the effect on strips.
- Ship paper components inside a heat-sealed foil or PE inner bag, separate from any odorous item.

### Gaps
- No HK-specific study of mold on boxed paper goods was found; thresholds are from conservation literature.
- No quantitative data on wool odor development in humid storage was found.
- No source was found for charcoal saturation rate in a sealed retail box.

## 4. Safety, allergy and HK regulatory considerations

### Takeaway
None of the candidates carries a serious safety risk at kit scale, with two exceptions. **Citrus peel or citrus oil on skin** carries a phototoxicity risk from furocoumarins. **Charcoal sachets** can shed fine black dust that stains. Wool "allergy" is largely irritation rather than true allergy. Including **coffee likely makes it "prepackaged food"** under HK law, which triggers full food labelling. Any safety warnings must be **bilingual (English and Chinese)** under the Consumer Goods Safety Regulation.

### Cited Findings
**Wool**
- Current evidence does not support wool fiber as a cutaneous allergen. Contact allergy to lanolin, chromium or formaldehyde from modern wool garments is highly unlikely. Wool does cause **irritant** reactions, particularly in atopic people. — [Zallmann et al. 2017, *Acta Derm Venereol* 97(8), "Debunking the Myth of Wool Allergy"](https://www.medicaljournals.se/acta/content/html/10.2340/00015555-2655)
- Lanolin (wool alcohols) contact allergy is found in about 0.4% of the general European population on TRUE test. It ranges from 1.2% to 6.9% in dermatitis patients. Higher-risk groups include people with atopic dermatitis, stasis dermatitis or leg ulcers. Lanolin was the American Contact Dermatitis Society's 2023 Allergen of the Year. — [Acta Derm Venereol review](https://www.medicaljournals.se/acta/content/html/10.2340/00015555-2655); [Dermatology Times 2023](https://www.dermatologytimes.com/view/lanolin-365-days-in-2023-s-allergen-of-the-year)

**Citrus**
- Bergamot, grapefruit, lemon, lime, mandarin and bitter orange peel oils contain phototoxic furocoumarins. IFRA restricts total bergapten-equivalents in leave-on skin products to **0.0015% (15 ppm)**. — [IFRA Standard (citrus/furocoumarins)](https://ifrafragrance.org/standards/IFRA_STD_089.pdf); [Wholesale Supplies Plus, "The science of citrus"](https://www.wholesalesuppliesplus.com/blogs/articles/the-science-of-citrus)

**HK food labelling (if coffee is included)**
- "Prepackaged food" is any food packaged so the contents cannot be altered without opening it and that is ready for presentation to the consumer as a single item. Under Cap. 132W Schedule 3, the label must show the food name, ingredient list (with allergens), "best before" or "use by" date, any special storage conditions, the manufacturer's or packer's full name and address, and net weight. A nutrition label is also required unless exempt. Labels can be in English, Chinese or both. Stick-on labels are accepted. — [CFS FAQ](https://cfs.gov.hk/english/faq/faq_03.html); [US Commercial Service HK labelling guide](https://www.trade.gov/knowledge-product/hong-kong-macau-labelingmarking-requirements)
- Penalties for false food labelling or non-compliance: up to HK$50,000 fine and 6 months imprisonment. — [CFS FAQ](https://cfs.gov.hk/english/faq/faq_03.html)

**General consumer goods safety**
- Under the Consumer Goods Safety Ordinance (Cap. 456), suppliers must ensure goods are "reasonably safe", having regard to presentation, instructions and warnings, published standards, and reasonable means to make them safer. — [HK Customs, Consumer Goods Safety](https://customs.gov.hk/en/consumer_protection/goods_safety/index.html); [CLIC](https://familyclic.hk/en/topics/daily-lives-legal-issues/consumer-rights/the-consumer-goods-safety-ordinance/)
- Under the Consumer Goods Safety Regulation (Cap. 456A, in force April 1998), any warning or caution about safe keeping, use, consumption or disposal **must appear in both English and Chinese**. — [CLIC](https://clic.org.hk/en/topics/consumer_complaints/consumer_protection/q4); [Customs slides](https://www.customs.gov.hk/hcms/filemanager/en/share/pdf/slides_relating_to_the_safety_of_consumer_goods_tc.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Coffee:** for most users the risks are not allergy but spill, mess, staining and pets (caffeine is toxic to dogs, unsourced here). Including edible-grade coffee likely triggers Cap. 132W labelling: best-before date, packer address, net weight, possibly a nutrition label. That means extra compliance work and a dated SKU. Labelling it "for scent only, not for consumption" may change its legal status but would need legal confirmation. It also still requires bilingual warnings under Cap. 456A.
- **Citrus:** avoid dried peel or citrus oil strips that users might rub on skin before going outdoors. If citrus is used at all, a sealed scented strip that is sniffed but never applied to skin minimises the phototoxicity concern. Still, it is the biggest cross-contamination risk.
- **Charcoal:** fine-dust shedding onto white strips, blotting sheets or the photo lid is a practical problem. Use a tightly woven or non-woven sealed sachet. Add a bilingual "do not open / not for consumption / keep away from children" warning.
- **Wool/cashmere:** low risk. Note "may irritate sensitive skin; sniff only". Cashmere has lower irritation potential (finer fiber; unsourced inference).
- **Own skin or plain air:** no safety or regulatory burden at all.

### Gaps
- I found no specific source on HK import licensing for small quantities of roasted coffee. The CFS and FEHD rules accessed cover labelling only. The [USDA FAIRS report for HK](https://agriexchange.apeda.gov.in/ImportRegulations/FoodandAgriculturalImportRegulationsandStandardsCountryReportHongKongHongKong06302021.pdf) is a likely next source; it was not fetched.
- I found no source on charcoal dust inhalation risk at sachet scale.
- I did not confirm whether Cap. 132W small-volume nutrition-label exemptions apply to a few grams of coffee in a gift kit.

## 6. Sustainability and premium-feel considerations

### Takeaway
Reusable, inert items (glazed ceramic or stone, a glass sniff cup) and the zero-item "own skin" ritual score best on sustainability and contamination. Coffee is perishable and needs a multilayer foil pouch. Charcoal sachets are often marketed as "eco" but are a consumable prop. A premium, honest kit can make the *pause* itself the designed object.

### Cited Findings
- Multilayer foil laminates (PET/AL/PE, OPP/AL/PE) are the standard high-barrier format. — [Smrke et al. 2022](https://digitalcollection.zhaw.ch/bitstream/11475/26687/3/2022_Smrke-etal_Effects-of-different-coffee-storage-methods-on-coffee-freshness_FPSL.pdf); [Walmart Muka listing](https://www.walmart.com/ip/5672176351)
- Charcoal sachets are marketed as fragrance-free, pet-safe and "reactivatable in sunlight" for up to two years. These are seller claims. — [UCHANGE](https://uchangepromo.com/products/nature-fresh-bamboo-charcoal-air-purifying-bags)

### Inferences
- Multilayer foil laminates are generally hard to recycle in most municipal streams (unsourced here). Each coffee or citrus pouch adds a non-recyclable component.
- Coffee and dried peel are perishable. Unsold kits need re-packing, or they ship a stale and arguably off-putting item, which is bad for premium perception.
- **Premium-feel ranking (judgement):** (1) glazed ceramic or stone "pause stone", or a small glass "fresh air" cup with lid, which is tactile, reusable, inert and photographable. (2) Cashmere swatch, which feels soft and luxurious but holds odor. (3) Coffee, which is recognisable but cliché and contradicted by evidence. (4) Charcoal sachet, which reads utilitarian or "shoe deodoriser". (5) Paper card and cotton pad, which feel generic.
- An honest, sustainable option: no physical reset item. Print a "pause" ritual on the lid or an insert. Optionally include a reusable inert object that doubles as a **strip stand**, so it has a real function beyond the ritual.

### Gaps
- No lifecycle or recyclability data specific to HK waste streams for foil laminates, charcoal sachets or ceramic were found.
- No consumer research on how premium buyers perceive coffee beans versus other reset props was found.

## 7. Honest copywriting and HK legal framing (Trade Descriptions Ordinance, etc.)

### Takeaway
Under HK's Trade Descriptions Ordinance (Cap. 362), any direct or indirect indication about goods that is false to a material degree, or **misleading even if not false**, is an offence. Penalties reach HK$500,000 and 5 years on indictment. Since evidence shows coffee and lemon are no better than plain air, phrases like "resets", "cleanses" or "neutralises your nose" are risky. Frame the item as a **ritual, pause or cue** and put the efficacy on the pause itself, which has some support. Avoid medical or "restore your sense of smell" language, which drifts toward the Undesirable Medical Advertisements Ordinance (Cap. 231).

### Cited Findings
- TDO s.2 defines "trade description" as an indication, direct or indirect and by whatever means given, about the goods or any part of them. A "false trade description" is one false to a material degree, or one that, though not false, is misleading. — [HK Customs TDO pamphlet (Unfair Trade Practices Amendment 2012)](https://www.customs.gov.hk/hcms/filemanager/en/content_201/tdo_pamphlet_en.pdf)
- s.7 makes it an offence to apply a false trade description to goods, supply them, or possess them for trade. — [Customs TDO pamphlet](https://www.customs.gov.hk/hcms/filemanager/en/content_201/tdo_pamphlet_en.pdf)
- s.13E covers **misleading omissions**: omitting or hiding material information, or giving it in an unclear, ambiguous or untimely way, judged against the "average consumer". — [Customs TDO pamphlet](https://www.customs.gov.hk/hcms/filemanager/en/content_201/tdo_pamphlet_en.pdf); [CLIC](https://clic.org.hk/en/topics/consumer_complaints/consumer_protection/q3)
- Penalties under s.18: on indictment, HK$500,000 and 5 years. On summary conviction, HK$100,000 and 2 years. Courts may also order compensation (s.18A). Customs is the principal enforcer. — [Customs TDO pamphlet](https://www.customs.gov.hk/hcms/filemanager/en/content_201/tdo_pamphlet_en.pdf)
- Customs can accept written undertakings under a civil compliance-based mechanism. A cosmetics example: a beauty centre with a suspected false organic logo on a skincare product. — [GovHK press release 2019](https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/201905/10/P2019051000560p.htm)
- The UMAO (Cap. 231) prohibits advertisements claiming a medicine, surgical appliance or treatment has curative or preventive properties for listed conditions. — [HK Health Bureau LegCo reply](https://www.healthbureau.gov.hk/en/legco/replies/2007/lq070509_q04.htm); [CMRO guideline notice](https://www.cmro.gov.hk/files/en/whats_new/letter_trader/2012/Commencement_of_the_Undesirable_Medical_Advertisements_Amendment_Ordinance_2005_Eng.pdf)
- Under the Consumer Goods Safety Regulation, warnings must be bilingual. — [CLIC](https://clic.org.hk/en/topics/consumer_complaints/consumer_protection/q4)

### Inferences: phrasing guidance (proposals, not sourced brand copy)
**Avoid (implied efficacy, potentially "misleading" under TDO s.2/s.7, or an omission under s.13E):**
- "Resets your nose", "cleanses your olfactory palate", "neutralises scent fatigue", "clinically proven", "restores your sense of smell", "absorbs perfume from your nose", "scientifically formulated reset".

**Safer ritual or pause framing:**
- "A pause between scents. Step away, breathe room air, and come back when you're ready."
- "Our between-scent ritual: set the strip down, hold the stone, take three slow breaths of plain air. Time away does the work. The stone just marks the moment."
- "Many perfumers smell the inside of their own wrist or elbow between scents. It's a familiar, neutral reference, and it's free." (Fine as an anecdote; avoid saying it is proven.)
- "Coffee beans are a perfume-counter tradition. Studies haven't shown they work better than fresh air, so we've left them out." This turns honesty into a brand signal and pre-empts misleading-omission concerns.
- If coffee or another prop *is* included: "Included as a sensory ritual, a familiar contrast note. It isn't a cleanser, and fresh air works just as well."
- Instructions with timing (evidence-consistent): "Wait at least 2-3 minutes between scents. If everything starts smelling the same, take a longer break (10-15 minutes)." Based on [Philpott et al. 2008](https://ueaeprints.uea.ac.uk/id/eprint/13438/), mean 170 s with a 15-minute clinical retest interval.
- Bilingual sachet or pouch warnings (Cap. 456A): "Not for consumption. Keep away from children and pets. Do not open." / 「不可食用。請放置於兒童及寵物接觸不到的地方。請勿打開。」 (translation should be checked by a native speaker).

### Gaps
- I found no published examples of fragrance or wellness brands' exact ritual-item copy that disclaims efficacy. The phrasings above are proposals.
- I did not verify whether HK's Advertising Standards (Communications Authority codes) apply to the kit's online marketing. They mainly cover broadcast.
- I did not confirm whether "loss of smell" or anosmia is among UMAO's scheduled conditions. The advice to avoid "restore your sense of smell" is precautionary.
- Not legal advice. A short review by HK counsel is recommended if the kit includes coffee (food labelling) or any functional claim.
