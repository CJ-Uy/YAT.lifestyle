# Unit Economics: Personalized Perfume Test Kit (Hong Kong-only shipping)

Research date: 2026-10-03. Currency: USD unless marked HK$. FX used for conversions: US$1 ≈ HK$7.8 (HKD is pegged in a 7.75–7.85 band; see [HKMA Linked Exchange Rate System](https://www.hkma.gov.hk/eng/key-functions/money/linked-exchange-rate/)). Anything labelled **EST** is my estimate or arithmetic, not a quoted price. Many supplier figures come from search-result snippets of marketplace listings (Alibaba, Global Sources, eBay), which change daily and are often list prices before freight, so treat them as ranges.

## 2. Perfume concentrate / small-batch EDP cost per ml; bottle + atomizer + cap

### Takeaway
Raw fragrance concentrate for a beginner or indie perfumer buying small lots runs about US$200–300/kg (≈US$0.20–0.30/g), or about US$0.50/g from hobby suppliers. A 50 ml EDP at 15–20% concentration therefore holds about US$1.5–5 of concentrate. Contract compounding and fill for 50 ml EDP is about US$4–9 per unit once minimums are met, and stock crimp-spray bottles cost about US$0.10–0.60 each wholesale. The real cost driver for a one-off personalized bottle is labour, not materials.

### Cited Findings
- Beginners buying materials in the smallest quantities typically land at US$200–300/kg of concentrate; indie perfumers who supply concentrate typically charge 3–5× raw material cost. — [Basenotes forum post](https://basenotes.com/goto/post?id=6192978) (forum opinion, not audited data)
- Small-quantity purchases from The Perfumer's Apprentice cost around US$0.50/g, about US$10 of materials per 100 ml bottle at 20% concentration. — [Basenotes forum post](https://basenotes.com/goto/post?id=6271923) (forum opinion)
- Single materials vary widely; Ambroxan can reach €700–1,000/kg retail. — [Basenotes forum post](https://basenotes.com/goto/post?id=6371352) (forum)
- Contract per-unit compounding and fill for 50 ml EDP is US$4–9 once minimums are cleared. Other figures from the same guide:
  - Formulation fee: US$500–2,500.
  - Sample rounds: US$150–600 each, 2–4 typical.
  - Compliance testing: US$300–1,200 per SKU.
  - MOQ: 300–500 for stock-base, 1,000+ for bespoke formulation.
  - Total cash to first pallet: US$9k–30k+.
  — [Closo, Sep 2026](https://closo.co/blogs/inventory-liquidation/fragrance-manufacturer)
- Designer fragrance retail-price split: juice 3–8%, bottle and packaging 10–15%, marketing 25–40%, distribution and retail 30–40%, brand profit 10–15%. For a US$70 perfume: fragrance oil US$1–3, alcohol and formulation US$2–5, packaging US$10–15. — [Premiere Peau](https://premierepeau.com/blogs/news/the-real-price-of-a-bottle-deconstruction-of-a-margin); [Sourcify](https://sourcify.com/why-fragrance-has-some-of-the-highest-margins-in-cpg/)
- Indie houses pay more for raw materials and packaging than designer brands, and natural-heavy formulas raise cost. — [Bois de Jasmin](https://boisdejasmin.com/?p=29)
- Wholesale spray perfume bottles run US$0.05–2.00/pc depending on MOQ (50–10,000). Examples: US$0.09–0.11 (MOQ 5,000), US$0.30–0.60 (MOQ 10), US$0.50–0.60 (MOQ 10,000). — [Global Sources perfume bottles](https://www.globalsources.com/category/perfume-bottles-for-sale-price_21737/?pageNum=7)
- South African retail packs: 50 ml glass crimp bottles, 70-pack, R431.90–776.30 (≈R6.2–11.1 each); 30 ml crimp bottles, 144-pack, R632.16–1,222.56 (≈R4.4–8.5 each), depending on closure. — [Bonpak 50 ml](https://bonpak.co.za/product/50ml-glass-perfume-crimp-bottle-3); [Bonpak 30 ml](https://bonpak.co.za/product/30ml-glass-perfume-crimp-bottle)
- Hand crimping of perfume bottles by small makers is discussed on Basenotes; it needs a crimping tool. — [Basenotes crimping thread](https://basenotes.com/threads/perfume-bottle-crimping.465012/post-4968840)

### Inferences
- **EST, juice cost per ml (EDP at 15–20%):** about US$0.03–0.06/ml for concentrate at US$200–300/kg (HK$0.25–0.50/ml), plus perfumer's alcohol. At US$0.50/g hobby prices this becomes US$0.08–0.10/ml.
- **EST, 50 ml personalized EDP bottle, all-in before shipping:**
  - Concentrate: HK$12–40.
  - Alcohol: HK$3–8 (unsourced).
  - Stock bottle, pump and cap: HK$2–16.
  - Box: HK$3–9.
  - Bilingual label: HK$1–3.
  - Hand compounding and QC labour for a one-off blend: HK$30–80, assuming 15–30 minutes at HK hourly cost (unsourced).
  - **Total ≈ HK$50–155.**
- **EST, 30 ml bottle:** roughly 60% of the juice cost and similar fixed costs, ≈ HK$40–130.
- **EST, 2 ml sample juice:** 2 ml at 20% is about 0.4 g of concentrate, ≈ US$0.08–0.20 (HK$0.6–1.6) per vial plus alcohol.
- Bespoke one-off bottles cannot use contract-filler MOQs. Personalization is economical only if built from a small library of pre-compounded accords blended by volume (EST/design inference).

### Gaps
- No HK price for perfumer's alcohol (SDA 40B-type or undenatured ethanol) delivered in HK; see the regulatory section for licensing implications.
- No data on HK lab or compounding labour rates for small fragrance makers.
- No sourced price for premium heavy-glass bottles with magnetic caps (indie "luxury" look), which can far exceed stock crimp bottles.

## 3. Hong Kong local delivery costs (SF Express, HK Post, Lalamove) and returns

### Takeaway
A small kit parcel within HK costs roughly HK$30–33 by SF Express (first 0.5 kg, plus about 10% fuel surcharge), HK$45+ by Lalamove motorcycle (on-demand, first 10 km), and probably less by HK Post local parcel. Carrier acceptance of alcohol-based perfume is the bigger issue: sources conflict on whether SF Express accepts it.

### Cited Findings
- SF Express domestic (HK→HK), 0.5 kg parcel: HK$30.00, 1–2 working days. — [Easyship SF Express Domestic](https://www.easyship.com/en-hk/couriers/shipping-sfexpress-domestic)
- SF HK local pricing: first 500 g about HK$30, each additional 500 g about HK$15. Possible fuel surcharge (~10%), remote-area surcharge (HK$20–50), and handling fees. Urban deliveries can arrive same day. — [Easyship SF Express Domestic (zh-hk)](https://www.easyship.com/zh-hk/couriers/shipping-sfexpress-domestic) (aggregator; the official SF rate page did not render on fetch)
- SF Local charges by volumetric weight (max 40 kg); HK Post Local Parcel charges by actual weight (max 20 kg). The Easyship comparison says "Liquids … are allowed" for both. — [Easyship SF vs HK Post comparison](https://www.easyship.com/en-hk/couriers/comparison/sfexpress-domestic-vs-hkpost-localparcel)
- Hongkong Post raised principal postage rates from 13 April 2026; local letter (≤30 g) went from HK$2.20 to HK$2.40. Over 90% of local and surface mail rises by ≤HK$0.20; local parcels ≤9 kg rise with inflation and >9 kg fall up to 28.6%. — [news.gov.hk, 23 Mar 2026](https://www.news.gov.hk/eng/2026/03/20260323/20260323_114133_671.html); [The Standard](https://www.thestandard.com.hk/news/article/327423/Hongkong-Post-announces-postage-rate-hike-starting-April-13)

  - A search summary said SF Express prohibits alcohol and restricts perfume as dangerous goods. — [Fuuffy shipping guide](https://www.fuuffy.com/en/article/Shipping%20Guide/163); [HAFFA](https://haffa.com.hk/en-HK/Page/Default.aspx?id=2902)

### Inferences
- **EST, shipping per order:**
  - Discovery kit (<0.5 kg): HK$30–35 by SF.
  - Lab kit (glass dishes and rigid box, likely 0.5–1.0 kg volumetric): HK$45–50 by SF.
  - Lalamove (HK$45+) suits only same-day premium delivery.
  - SF locker or pickup-point delivery may be cheaper; not verified.
- **EST, return logistics:** a return label costs about one outbound fare (HK$30–50). Budget returns at 4–10% × (shipping + unsellable opened kit). Opened kits and personalized bottles are effectively unresaleable, so a "no returns on personalized" policy plus breakage replacement is typical (inference).
- If carriers refuse alcohol-based EDP, options are: self-delivery, Lalamove courier (ground, but check its own restricted items), an SF ground DG arrangement, or customer pickup. All add cost.

## 4. Typical gross margins for indie/niche fragrance and DTC beauty brands

### Takeaway
DTC beauty brands typically run 65–72% gross margin (median about 69%), and fragrance and prestige sit at 80–85%. After fulfilment, shipping and returns, CM2 is about 52–62%; after CAC, CM3 is about 25–35%. First orders are often loss-making, with repeat purchase carrying the profit.

### Cited Findings
- DTC beauty/fragrance benchmarks (Jun 2026):
  - Gross margin 65–72%, median 69%.
  - CM2 (after payment, fulfilment, shipping, returns) 52–62%, median about 57%.
  - CM3 (after CAC) 25–35%, median about 30%.
  - CAC US$40–100+, median US$61.
  - AOV US$55–80, median US$66.
  - 12-month LTV US$150–250.
  - Annual repeat rate 30–45%.
  - Returns 4–10%.
  - "CM3 on that first order is around minus $4." — [eightx beauty unit economics](https://eightx.co/blog/beauty-brand-unit-economics)
- Perfume can reach 85% gross margin; prestige/luxury runs 70–85%+, masstige 60–75%, mass/drugstore 40–60%. The 2026 median beauty gross margin across e.l.f. (71.2%), Olaplex (69.4%) and Beauty Health (65.3%) is 69.4%. — [eightx beauty margin benchmarks](https://eightx.co/blog/beauty-ecommerce-margin-benchmarks)
- Designer fragrance: juice only 3–8% of retail; brand keeps 10–15% operating profit after a 30–40% retail margin and 25–40% marketing. — [Premiere Peau](https://premierepeau.com/blogs/news/the-real-price-of-a-bottle-deconstruction-of-a-margin)
- Fragrance is among the highest-margin CPG categories. — [Sourcify](https://sourcify.com/why-fragrance-has-some-of-the-highest-margins-in-cpg/)

### Inferences
- **EST pricing implications (target ~70% gross margin on goods, shipping excluded):**
  - Discovery kit COGS HK$15–30 (with juice) supports a price of about HK$60–120.
  - Lab kit COGS HK$60–130 supports about HK$250–450.
  - 50 ml personalized EDP at HK$50–155 supports about HK$350–700. HK niche EDP retail is often higher; benchmark against the competitor notes.
- Crediting the kit price toward the bottle shifts margin: the effective bottle revenue is (bottle price − kit credit). If the full kit price is credited, kit COGS and shipping become acquisition cost recovered only on conversion. The kit-to-bottle conversion rate becomes the key driver (inference, consistent with eightx's observation that first orders lose money).

### Gaps
- No HK-specific indie fragrance margin data was found; benchmarks are US/global DTC.
- No published kit-to-full-bottle conversion rates for sample-kit fragrance brands (e.g. credit-back models) were found.

## 5. Regulatory and cost implications for selling fragrance/cosmetics in Hong Kong

### Takeaway
HK has no cosmetics pre-market registration or notification. Perfume falls under the general safety requirement of the Consumer Goods Safety Ordinance (Cap 456), and any warning or caution must appear in both English and Chinese, legibly and conspicuously. Two compliance cost items follow: bilingual labels and safety-substantiation documents such as lab certificates, which support the due-diligence defence. Separately, ethanol supply may touch the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance.

### Cited Findings
- **CGSO (Cap 456):**
  - Manufacturers, importers and suppliers must ensure consumer goods are reasonably safe.
  - Warnings and cautions on safe keeping, use, consumption or disposal must be in both English and Chinese, legible and conspicuous, on the product, packaging or enclosed documentation.
  - Customs and Excise enforces.
  - Defence: "took all reasonable steps and exercised all due diligence," e.g. lab certificates.
  — [HK Customs, goods safety](https://www.customs.gov.hk/en/service-enforcement-information/consumer-protection/goods-safety/index.html)
- Selling unsafe goods carries a fine and/or imprisonment; unsafe goods can be seized. — [HK Customs goods safety (via search)](https://www.customs.gov.hk/en/consumer_protection/goods_safety/index.html); [FamilyClic, CGSO guide](https://familyclic.hk/en/topics/daily-lives-legal-issues/consumer-rights/the-consumer-goods-safety-ordinance/)
- Cosmetics must meet safety standards recognised by national or international standards bodies. The CGSO does not set comprehensive cosmetic labelling (e.g. full INCI), and there is no mandatory pre-market registration. Claims must not be inaccurate or misleading. — [Freyr, HK cosmetic regulation](https://www.freyrsolutions.com/blog/navigating-cosmetic-regulatory-standards-in-hong-kong-a-brief-analysis); [Govt LCQ8, safety of cosmetic products](https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/200611/01/P200611010113.htm)
- Consumer Goods Safety Regulation (Cap 456A) has been in operation since April 1998. — [HK Customs (via search)](https://www.customs.gov.hk/en/consumer_protection/goods_safety/index.html)
- **Dutiable Commodities Ordinance (Cap 109):**
  - "Liquor" covers liquids with more than 1.2% ethyl alcohol by volume, except denatured spirits and certain ingredients of other goods.
  - Importing liquor above 30% ABV requires an importer licence and removal permits, applied for through the Dutiable Commodities System.
  — [HKTDC Wine Fair circular](https://hkwinefair.hktdc.com/pdf/2024/Circular/Circular_06.pdf); [WTO import licensing, HK dutiable commodities](https://importlicensing.wto.org/content/dutiable-commodities); [GovHK how to apply](https://www.info.gov.hk/info/howto/dutiable-e.htm)

### Inferences
- **EST, compliance cost:**
  - Bilingual flammability/eye-contact/patch-test cautions on every vial, card and bottle label: negligible per unit, but needs a design pass.
  - Safety assessment / IFRA conformity documents per base accord: about US$300–1,200 per SKU, borrowing Closo's compliance testing range.
  - Optional product liability insurance (unpriced).
- Buying denatured perfumer's alcohol (e.g. SDA 40B) likely sidesteps the liquor-licensing regime, since denatured spirits are excluded from "liquor". This needs verification with HK Customs (inference).
- The kit includes pH strips and skin-contact activities (oil blotting, pH reading). Instructions should carry bilingual cautions, and claims about "skin chemistry matching" should avoid being misleading (inference from the misleading-claims point and the Trade Descriptions principle).
- Storing bulk ethanol-based stock may trigger Fire Services dangerous-goods storage limits (unverified; see Gaps).

### Gaps
- Exact CGSO penalty amounts were not captured from a primary source.
- Fire Services Department dangerous goods (Cap 295) exempt quantities for storing ethanol-based perfume or alcohol in a small workshop were not researched or sourced.
- No confirmation on whether HK requires IFRA or EU-style allergen labelling (not mandatory per the sources above, but retailers may require it).
